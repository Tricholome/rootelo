The Woodland Cartography

While the Leaderboard tracks individual skill through Elo ratings, Homelands maps the social fabric of the league. Recurring playgroups naturally crystallize into clearings rallied around an iconic woodland item, sized by their population of active denizens and linked by dashed paths. Use the bottom date controls to navigate day by day through the season and watch homelands emerge, grow, or fade away.

The Internal Constellation

Within each homeland, denizens are shaped by two distinct metrics. Weight reflects the volume of recent matches played with fellow denizens, dictating bubble size and proximity to the homeland's core. Fidelity measures the share of games played inside this homeland versus others, shading bubbles from Friend (< 50%) to Squire (50%–80%) and Partisan (> 80%). These metrics operate independently: a denizen may hold substantial weight despite fluctuating fidelity, and vice versa.

The Player’s Circle

Clicking any denizen bubble reveals their personal network across the entire season. The more matches shared with a companion, the larger their bubble grows and the closer they orbit the center. Bubble colors distinguish partners belonging to the same homeland, another homeland, or wandering unaligned. On the right, the Allegiances ledger lets you revisit every homeland the player has belonged to during the season.

The Louvain Compass

Under the hood, the Woodland is modeled as a dynamic mathematical graph where players are nodes and shared matches form weighted trails between them. Every active day, the engine charts communities using the Louvain method—a modularity optimization algorithm designed to uncover natural clusters inside complex networks.

Rather than forcing fixed factions, the algorithm compares the density of games played inside a group against the games expected by pure chance across the league. When a circle of active competitors shares significantly more tables together than with the rest of the forest, a Homeland naturally crystallizes.

The Laws of Emergence

To prevent the map from flickering erratically after every match while still reflecting shifting seasonal habits, the simulation enforces four physical rules:

Trail Decay −5% / day γ = 0.95 Every match adds 1.0 weight to a pair's bond, while existing bonds decay by 5% each active match day so recent fellowships outweigh old history.
Foundation Birth & Cap 6+ Players Up to 5 clearings can coexist. A new clearing requires at least 6 eligible active players with dense internal ties to claim a woodland item.
Stickiness Inertia ×1.5 Pull A player only transfers to a rival clearing if their pull toward the new group exceeds their current homeland bond by at least 50% (max 3 transfers/day).
Dissolution Survival Floor < 4 Players Players inactive for 21 days return to the unaligned wilds. If a clearing drops below 4 active denizens, it dissolves—though it can be Revived if 3 former members reunite.

The Forces of the Woodland

Left unchecked, a pure mathematical compass would flicker after every match or merge the entire league into a single giant clearing. To give the map a natural rhythm, four counterbalancing forces shape the lifecycle of homelands:

Overgrowth Fading Trails Every shared match carves a deeper trail between companions, while neglected paths slowly fade back into the brush. Denizens who rest for too long or fall behind the seasonal pace eventually drift back into the unaligned wilds.
Cohesion Critical Mass A clearing only takes root—or reclaims a long-abandoned woodland item—when enough seasoned denizens form a tight circle clearly distinct from existing groups. If a homeland's active core thins out too much, it dissolves back into the forest.
Stickiness Allegiance Inertia Once settled, denizens remain rooted in their clearing rather than drifting at the slightest whim. Defecting to a rival homeland requires a decisively stronger pull, and daily migrations are throttled so allegiances shift gradually.
Crowding Canopy Balance Space in the Woodland is finite. Each active homeland raises the bar required to found or sustain another, while oversized clearings have their gravitational pull dampened so they cannot effortlessly swallow smaller neighbors.
